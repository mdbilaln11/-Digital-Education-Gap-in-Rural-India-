**AI-Powered Data Analysis Report: Digital Education in Rural India**

# Project Overview #

This project was completed as part of my Data Analytics Internship at
InAmigos Foundation.

For this task, I explored the digital education gap in rural India. The
report looks at smartphone access, personal device ownership,
differences between boys and girls, and how digital technology is being
used for education.

The purpose of this project is to understand how AI tools can support
research, organise information, summarise findings, and help turn data
into practical recommendations.

Research Topic

Digital Education in Rural India: Understanding the Digital Gap and
How NGOs Can Help

Key Findings

The report uses findings from the ASER 2024 survey of rural children
aged 14--16.

89.1% reported having a smartphone at home.

82.2% reported knowing how to use a smartphone.

31.4% of children who could use a smartphone reported owning one
themselves.

57% reported using a smartphone for an educational activity in
the previous week.

76% reported using a smartphone for social media in the previous
week.

Personal smartphone ownership was reported by 36.2% of boys and
26.9% of girls in the relevant group.

These figures suggest that having a smartphone in a household does not
necessarily mean every student has personal or independent access to it.
The difference in reported ownership between boys and girls also
highlights the importance of inclusive digital learning opportunities.

Main Challenges Identified

Limited personal access to smartphones and computers.

Unequal access to technology for girls and boys.

A need for stronger digital and learning skills.

Online safety and digital awareness.

Connectivity, language, and access barriers in some communities.

Suggested Solutions for NGOs

Set up community digital-learning spaces with shared computers or
tablets.

Organise inclusive digital literacy sessions and ensure equal
participation.

Teach students how to use educational websites, apps, and online
learning resources.

Provide awareness sessions on privacy, passwords, scams, and safe
internet use.

Offer downloadable or offline learning materials and local-language
resources.

Track programme participation and students' digital skills to
measure progress.

How AI Was Used

ChatGPT was used as a support tool to:

Organise the research topic and report structure.

Summarise information from published sources.

Help interpret the reported statistics.

Develop possible recommendations based on the findings.

Improve clarity and readability.

AI-generated content was not treated as a primary source. The statistics
are attributed to the publications listed below, and recommendations are
presented as proposed actions rather than survey results.
